ENGR 1166: Foundations of Engineering · University of Connecticut · Spring 2024

Mars Rover —
Housing & Circuit Design

Designed and built the housing and electronics for an autonomous rover prototype — laser-cut plywood enclosure with a color-coded sensor system, mini solar panel for rover recharging, and Arduino-based control circuit.

circuits CAD laser-cut Arduino
01 — Overview

The challenge

ENGR 1166 Foundations of Engineering was a first-year design course at UConn taken spring 2024. The semester project was a large collaborative diorama simulating a Mars habitat, built across 7 or 8 separate groups. Each group was responsible for one component of the scene. One group built the rover. Another built the tiny house. My team built the garage, which sat between them and housed the solar panels that the rover would use to recharge.

This meant two layers of coordination: working within our own team to design and build the garage, while also working across teams to make sure everything physically and electrically connected. Dimensions, panel placement, and power connections all had to be negotiated with the house group and the rover group throughout the semester. Nothing could be finalized in isolation.

02 — Housing Design

The garage structure

The garage enclosure was designed using CAD and laser-cut from plywood using finger-joint construction, which kept the structure rigid without needing additional fasteners at the corners. The two-tier build separated the main electronics compartment on top from the lower bay. Exterior dimensions had to be coordinated with the house team so the two units could physically connect at a shared wall.

Access doors on two sides let components be reached after assembly. The doors were color-coded with yarn stitching in blue, pink, and red to indicate sensor orientation and make assembly less error-prone without having to refer back to documentation every time.

The solar panel interface was a key constraint. The panels were shared between the garage and the rover, so the garage needed mounting points and wiring that the rover team could connect to. This required several rounds of back-and-forth to get the connector placement right.

The mini solar panel mounted on top of the garage served as the power source for the rover recharging interface. Cutouts were sized to fit sensors flush with the outer face. The finger joints were glued and clamped during assembly. Routing wiring cleanly inside a tight two-tier box ended up being harder than expected and is the main thing I would plan better next time.

both units side by side, electronics visible on top

03 — Electronics

Circuit and wiring

The control circuit was built around an Arduino and wired on a breadboard. The Solar panel wiring ran from the panel on top down through the enclosure to a shared connection point the rover team could tap into for recharging. Sensors were routed through the side access doors into the electronics compartment.

The circuit went through a few iterations. Early versions had loose connections that caused intermittent behavior, which we fixed by soldering key junctions and securing the breadboard to the interior wall. Getting the power routing right for the shared solar interface required testing with the rover team to make sure current was actually passing correctly between the two units.

04 — Results

Outcome

The garage connected to the house as planned and the solar interface with the rover team worked. The finger-joint construction held up better than I expected for a first attempt at laser-cut enclosures. The color-coded doors turned out to be genuinely useful during both assembly and the final demo since they made sensor orientation immediately clear without having to check documentation.

The hardest part of the project was not the building itself but the coordination. Any change to the garage dimensions or connection points had downstream effects on both the house and the rover, which meant communication between teams had to stay constant throughout. It was a good lesson in how design constraints in one component can ripple through a larger system.
